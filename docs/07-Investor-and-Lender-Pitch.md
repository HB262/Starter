# Investor and lender pitch (outline, 12 slides)

*Draft 2, October 30, 2026. Use this to brief investors and lenders. A designed slide deck can be built from this outline on request. Every investor conversation must follow securities counsel's guidance (Reg D). Use 506(b) unless counsel advises otherwise: no public advertising.*

1. **The opportunity.** Fractional jet programs promise owners guaranteed lift. On peak days they buy overflow from vetted operators. The Citation Latitude is NetJets' midsize workhorse.
2. **Our model.** One Latitude based in Denver (KBJC), 100% charter, on a top-rated Part 135 certificate. It flies NetJets/fractional overflow plus broker charter. There is no owner use.
3. **The team.**
   - Henry: founder and sponsor; governance and investor relations.
   - Chief Pilot partner: Latitude type-rated and current at a comparable operator; runs day-to-day operations.
   - Advisers: aviation attorney, securities counsel, CPA, management company.
4. **Why Denver.** Ski and resort demand, central repositioning, and a high-altitude mission that suits the Latitude. A new Jet Aviation FBO/hangar campus is coming to KBJC in 2028.
5. **Unit economics, honestly.** One jet, mature year (base): $4.65M revenue and $0.50M EBITDA. Breakeven is about 430 billable hours before debt and 840 including debt. Show the hours sensitivity table. The purchase depends on a committed-hours letter.
6. **Where the value comes from.** Show the three-path comparison: management-led growth returns 3.6x on $8.6M, while owning more jets returns 0.5x in base. We build a management platform, and the owned jet is the anchor.
7. **The tax feature.** 100% bonus depreciation gives about $3.20 of first-year deductions per $1 invested at 80% LTV. It is best for investors with passive income. It is recaptured at sale, so it is a timing benefit. Investors should consult their own CPA.
8. **Risk controls.**
   - Committed-hours letter before purchase.
   - Written stop-loss: sell Jet #1 if cash after debt is worse than ($750K) a year by mid-2029.
   - Engines on program and a 6-month reserve.
   - Investor consent rights.
   - Dual-path certificate strategy.
9. **Terms.**
   - Fund I LLC; Class A units for investors.
   - Return of capital, then an 8% compounding pref, then an 80/20 split.
   - 1% acquisition fee; no ongoing asset-management fee.
   - Minimum ticket $250K (proposed).
10. **The raise.** About $4.2M at launch (80% LTV), with committed capital of up to about $8.6M over 3 years for the management-led plan. Use of funds: down payment, onboarding, refurb, reserve, early operating losses, certificate build-out.
11. **Returns.** Investor multiple by scenario (management-led): downside 2.2x, base 3.6x, upside 11x. Pre-tax IRR base 17%; about 30% if losses are usable.
12. **Timeline.** Investors soft-circled Feb 2027; close May 2027; revenue July 2027; own certificate 2030; exit 2034-2036.

## Lender ask (separate conversation)
- About $10.8M senior loan (80% of $13.5M), fixed rate, 15-20 year amortization. Expect a request for a guarantor or a lower LTV.
- Offer: an anchor-investor guarantee, a 6-month cash reserve, the management company's committed-hours letter, and engines on ESP.
