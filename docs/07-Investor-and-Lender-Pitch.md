# Investor and lender pitch (outline, 10 slides)

*Draft 1, October 30, 2026. Use this to brief lenders and any minority equity partner. A designed slide deck can be built from this outline on request.*

1. **The opportunity.** Fractional jet programs promise owners guaranteed lift. On peak days they buy overflow from vetted operators. The Citation Latitude is NetJets' midsize workhorse.
2. **Our model.** One Latitude based in Denver (KBJC), 100% charter, on a top-rated Part 135 certificate. It flies NetJets/fractional overflow plus broker charter. There is no owner use.
3. **The team.** Henry: founder, capital, governance. Chief Pilot partner: Latitude type-rated and current at a comparable operator; runs day-to-day operations. Advisers: aviation attorney, CPA, management company.
4. **Why Denver.** Ski and resort demand, central repositioning, and a high-altitude airport mission that suits the Latitude. A new Jet Aviation FBO/hangar campus is coming to KBJC in 2028.
5. **Unit economics.** One jet, mature year (base): $4.65M revenue, $0.51M EBITDA, breakeven about 425 billable hours before debt and about 840 including debt. Show the hours sensitivity table.
6. **Risk controls.** A committed-hours letter before purchase; stop-loss rule; engines on program; a 6-month reserve; dual-path certificate strategy.
7. **Financing ask.**
   - Lender: about $10.8M senior loan (80% of $13.5M), fixed rate, 15-20 year amortization. Personal guarantee and liquidity available (Henry to confirm).
   - Equity: about $4.0M total at launch.
8. **Growth plan.** Own Part 135 certificate in 2030. Grow to 3 owned and 12 managed aircraft by 2036, with revenue of about $21M and EBITDA of about $5.6M (base).
9. **Exit.** Sale to a consolidator in 2034-2036. Base equity value about $32M; upside about $77M.
10. **Use of funds and timeline.** Close May 2027; revenue July 2027; milestones and gates as in the roadmap.
